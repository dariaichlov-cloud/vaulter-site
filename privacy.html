Privacy

Last updated 13 September 2026

Your vault lives on your phone. There is no Vaulter account, no Vaulter server holding your things, and no way for anyone — including us — to look inside what you keep.

Two things do leave, and both are listed below in full: the catch itself, once, so it can be read; and anonymous counts, which you can switch off.

What stays on your phone

Everything you save. Titles, notes, pictures, links, tags, vaults, reminders, the text Vaulter read out of an image — all of it is stored in the app's own storage on your device and nowhere else.

It is not synced, not backed up by us, and not recoverable by us. If you delete the app, it is gone. That is also why Vaulter keeps asking you to back it up: yours is the only copy.

Reading a catch

Vaulter cannot understand a screenshot on its own. To read one, it sends that single catch to Anthropic's API, gets back a title, a description and some tags, and keeps the answer on your phone. The same happens with text and PDFs. Voice search works the same way through OpenAI's transcription API.

Vaulter keeps nothing on either service. There is no account there that belongs to you, nothing is stored under your name, and nothing sent for reading is kept by us anywhere. How those companies handle what reaches them is governed by their own terms — Anthropic's are at anthropic.com/legal/privacy and OpenAI's at openai.com/policies/privacy-policy.

If a catch is something you would rather no service ever saw, the honest answer is not to hand it to Vaulter.

Links

When you save a link, Vaulter asks that address for its title and preview picture — the same request your browser would make if you opened it. The site therefore sees a visit. Vaulter sends it nothing about you: no name, no identifier, no record of what else is in your vault.

The counts

Vaulter sends a small number of anonymous counts so we can tell what is working and what is broken. It is on by default, it is said plainly on the first screen, and the switch that turns it off is in Settings under Help fix Vaulter.

What is counted: that the app was opened; that a catch was started, saved, or failed; which of Vaulter's own vaults it went to; whether a search came back empty; and how old a catch was when you opened it again.

What is never sent: titles, notes, links, pictures, tags, the name you gave a vault, or anything you typed into search. Not "sent privately" — never sent. One small file in the app does all the sending, and the only things it is able to put in a message are numbers, yes/no, and words from a fixed list. Nothing you write has a route through it.

The counts are tied to a random number generated on your phone when you install the app. It is not your name, your email, your Apple or Google account, or your device's identifier, and it is not the advertising identifier. Deleting the app deletes it. The counts are processed by PostHog on servers in Frankfurt.

Permissions

  • Photos — so you can hand Vaulter a screenshot. It sees the ones you pick and nothing else.
  • Microphone — only while you are searching by voice.
  • Notifications — to remind you about something you saved. The reminders are scheduled on your phone, not sent from anywhere.
  • Calendar — only when you ask Vaulter to add an event, and only to write that one event.

What Vaulter never does

  • No ads, and no advertising identifiers.
  • Nothing is sold, rented, or shared with data brokers.
  • You are not tracked across other apps or websites.
  • There is no feed, no sharing, and no way for other people to see what you keep.
  • Nobody at Vaulter can read your vault, because nobody has a copy of it.

Sharing a page

When you share catches as a page, that page is a file. It travels straight from your phone to whoever you send it to. It is not uploaded, it is not hosted by us, and we never see what is in it.

Children

Vaulter is not built for or aimed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect anything from them.

Changes

If what the app sends ever changes, this page changes with it, and the date at the top moves. The rule we hold ourselves to is that the table above is complete on the day each version ships.
